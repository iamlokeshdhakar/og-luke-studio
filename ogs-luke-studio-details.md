# 1. Brand & Business Definition

## 1.1 Brand Name

**CONFIRMED**

- **Primary name:** OG's LUXE Studios
- **Short name:** OG's LUXE
- **Primary category:** Premium photography, video, content-creation, and creative studio rental
- **Core business type:** Physical creative-space rental with curated visual sets

OG's LUXE Studios is **not** a music/audio recording studio, apartment/flat rental, coworking office, wedding banquet hall, generic event venue, or traditional photography shop.

## 1.2 One-Line Description

**OG's LUXE Studios is a premium, design-led studio rental space where photographers, creators, models, brands, agencies, and production teams can create photography, video, campaigns, products, fashion, e-commerce, and social content across multiple curated visual sets.**

## 1.3 Short Description

OG's LUXE Studios is a modern creative studio built around one simple idea: creators should be able to walk into a beautiful, camera-ready environment and start producing immediately.

Instead of offering one empty room, the studio is designed as a collection of **curated sets, styled corners, architectural backdrops, natural-light environments, and flexible production areas** under one roof.

The business should feel less like renting a room and more like entering a **creative visual world** designed to make content look polished, distinctive, and premium.

## 1.4 Core Positioning Statement

> **A premium visual playground for photography, video, fashion, products, campaigns, and modern content creation.**

The studio should be:

- Premium but approachable
- Design-led but practical
- Youthful but not childish
- Editorial but not overly formal
- Highly visual but easy to use
- Flexible enough for creators, brands, and production teams

# 2. Why This Business Exists

## 2.1 Market Context

**RESEARCH CONTEXT — not company claims**

India's creator economy is large and expanding. A 2025 BCG report estimated **2–2.5 million monetized creators in India**, influencing roughly **$350–400 billion in consumer spending**, with creator-influenced consumption projected to exceed $1 trillion by 2030.

Industry research reported by IBEF in 2025 also projected India's influencer marketing industry to grow **25% in 2025** from approximately ₹3,600 crore in 2024.

This matters to OG's LUXE because modern brands and creators need a constant supply of professional visual content — not just one annual campaign. They increasingly need repeatable places for reels, product launches, fashion, e-commerce, UGC, founder content, short-form video, and campaign assets.

## 2.2 Customer Problems

### Problem 1 — Generic studios look interchangeable

Many spaces are functional but visually forgettable.

**OG's LUXE answer:** curated, recognizable environments that already look good on camera.

### Problem 2 — One shoot needs many looks

Creators and brands often need multiple outputs for different platforms, outfits, products, or campaigns.

**OG's LUXE answer:** multiple distinct sets in one location.

### Problem 3 — Set building wastes booking time

Clients may lose time sourcing furniture, styling a blank room, moving props, or fixing unattractive backgrounds.

**OG's LUXE answer:** production-ready visual environments that reduce setup friction.

### Problem 4 — Brands need higher perceived production value

Growing brands want content that looks more considered than home, office, or basic backdrop shoots.

**OG's LUXE answer:** architecture, furniture, light, and styling that naturally elevate the frame.

### Problem 5 — Creators need repeatable content infrastructure

Creators and D2C teams need new content every week or month.

**OG's LUXE answer:** become a recurring creative home, not a one-time venue.

### Problem 6 — Booking studio space is often unclear

Clients frequently need to ask what is included, whether lights are available, how many people can come, whether furniture can move, whether video/audio is suitable, and what happens with overtime.

**OG's LUXE answer:** transparent set pages, inclusions, rules, add-ons, availability, and booking terms.

## 3.2 Offer Architecture

The business should clearly distinguish **space rental** from **production services**.

### A. Studio-Only / Dry Hire

**RECOMMENDED product tier**

Customer brings their own photographer, camera, crew, styling, and advanced equipment.

Could include:

- Booked space/set access
- Standard furniture shown in listing photos
- Basic utilities
- Basic set reset before arrival
- Standard props defined as included
- Wi-Fi / AC / restroom where available

### B. Studio + Equipment

**RECOMMENDED product tier**

Adds selected lighting, modifiers, backdrops, stands, or other production tools.

### C. Studio + Operator / Assistant

**RECOMMENDED product tier**

Adds basic technical help such as studio assistant, lighting setup support, or camera operator if offered.

### D. Managed Production

**OPTIONAL / FUTURE**

A higher-value package where OG's LUXE coordinates some or all of:

- Photographer/videographer
- Camera operator
- Lighting technician
- Creative direction
- Styling
- Hair and makeup
- Editing
- Short-form video deliverables
- Campaign production

This should be offered only if the team can deliver it reliably. Do not blur the line between rental and full production.

### 3. Fashion / Beauty Brand

**Needs:** campaign visuals, lookbooks, social media, launch content, model shoots.

### 4. D2C / E-Commerce Brand

**Needs:** product photos, product videos, model shots, short-form ads, repeatable content batches.

**Values:** repeat bookings, consistent lighting, props, product tables, predictable pricing, efficient throughput.

### 5. Creative / Marketing Agency

**Needs:** client-ready location, multiple visual options, professional operations, reliable booking.

**Values:** invoices, predictable policies, crew support, full-day bookings, client comfort, confidentiality.

### 6. Video Production Team

**Needs:** controllable light, power, space for gear, sound conditions, load-in, crew capacity.

**Values:** noise disclosure, power specs, dimensions, parking, rules for haze/special effects.

### 7. Model / Actor / Portfolio Customer

**Needs:** several looks, changing space, makeup area, photographer access.

**Values:** visually different sets, privacy, comfort, simple packaged booking.

### 8. Founder / Professional / Educator

**Needs:** personal-brand photography, interviews, thought-leadership video, course/social content.

**Values:** quiet environment, simple setup, professional finish, optional production help.

# 7. Production Infrastructure & Amenities

Research across studio-rental platforms and Indian studio operators shows that professional renters evaluate much more than the backdrop itself. The following information should be intentionally designed and documented.

## 7.1 Essential Infrastructure Checklist

**TO FINALIZE / BUILD**

- Air conditioning / ventilation
- Reliable electrical supply
- Power backup
- Number and location of power outlets
- High-speed Wi-Fi
- Restroom
- Hair and makeup area
- Full-length mirror
- Clothing rack / hangers
- Changing room / privacy zone
- Client seating / waiting area
- Drinking water
- Gear staging area
- Storage for cases/bags
- Loading route / lift / stairs
- Parking availability
- Vehicle loading/unloading rules
- Accessible entry where feasible
- Fire safety equipment
- First-aid kit
- CCTV/privacy policy
- Lockable storage if offered

## 7.2 Video-Specific Information

Every set page should state:

- Is the space soundproof? Yes / No
- Typical ambient-noise sources
- Is AC noise audible?
- Can AC be temporarily switched off?
- Street/construction/neighbor noise risks
- Echo/reverb characteristics
- Whether dialogue recording is recommended
- Whether blackout curtains/light control exist
- Maximum electrical load / available circuits if known

Never market a set as soundproof unless it is genuinely acoustically isolated.

## 7.3 Natural Light Information

For each set:

- Window direction if known
- Best time for direct/soft light
- Whether natural light can be blocked
- Example images at different times of day

This can become a strong merchandising advantage.

# 9. Pricing Strategy Principles

Indian studio operators commonly combine hourly rental, minimum booking windows, half-day/full-day options, set-specific rates, equipment add-ons, and recurring creator/brand plans.

## 9.1 Recommended Pricing Architecture

- Base hourly rate
- Minimum booking duration
- 4-hour / half-day package
- 8-hour / full-day package
- Single-set rate
- Full-studio / all-set rate
- Peak vs off-peak pricing if demand supports it
- Add-ons priced separately
- Overtime in fixed increments
- Monthly creator/brand/agency plans

## 9.2 Pricing Principles

- Show customers exactly what is included.
- Avoid surprise mandatory fees.
- Distinguish basic amenities from premium add-ons.
- Use clear GST language when applicable.
- State whether setup and teardown are inside the booked time.
- State whether the customer pays for only shooting time or access time.
- Do not compete only on the cheapest hourly price; sell **visual value + convenience + multiple looks + professional experience**.

## 9.3 Metrics Needed Before Final Pricing

- Fixed monthly facility cost
- Staff cost
- Cleaning/reset cost
- Utility cost
- Equipment depreciation
- Set maintenance/replacement cost
- Marketing/CAC
- Desired gross margin
- Target utilization
- Average booking duration
- Competitor rates in the actual city

# 13. Brand Identity System

| Color | Hex | Primary Role |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Forest Green | `#163B33` | Primary brand color, dark surfaces, signage, buttons, key backgrounds |
| Warm Luxe Gold | `#C8934E` | Accent, linework, highlights, premium details |
| Soft Ivory Beige | `#F3E7D7` | Main neutral, whitespace, light surfaces |

Do not introduce new dominant brand colors without a deliberate identity update.

## 13.2 Supportive Graphic Language — CONFIRMED

- Repeating arch outlines
- Architectural portal shapes
- Vertical studio slats
- Overlapping green/gold circles
- Thin gold rules
- Curved cutouts
- Warm natural-light shadows
- Minimal geometric pattern tiles

## 13.3 Typography

- **Primary display:** Elegant editorial serif
- **Secondary / utility:** Clean sans serif

Use serif type for emotional statements, major headlines, and premium storytelling. Use sans serif for navigation, labels, buttons, metadata, prices, rules, and body copy.

## 13.4 Logo Usage Rules

**RECOMMENDED to formally document**

- Primary full logo
- Simplified emblem
- Square/app icon
- One-color dark version
- One-color light version
- Minimum digital size
- Minimum print size
- Clear-space rule
- Approved backgrounds
- Forbidden treatments

Never stretch, redraw, recolor, outline, add random gradients, alter the letterforms, or add decorative borders to the approved logo.
